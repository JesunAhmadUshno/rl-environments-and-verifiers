# REPORT: rl-environments-and-verifiers (P26)

Status: exploratory. Every number below is read from a committed results
file and regenerated by the runners in this repo (README, Reproduce).
No proposal was locked before these results existed, so nothing here is a
confirmatory result (PROPOSAL.md says what a confirmatory cycle needs).

## Scope, stated first

This is a teaching analogue of GRPO and RLVR. A tabular softmax policy on
four tiny deterministic tasks, reward read only from replay verifiers, no
language model, no KL penalty, no clipping, no reference policy. Every
stage is dependency-free Python standard library, run on the Muse VM CPU
(Python 3.12). Reward comes only from replay verifiers, never from
hand-shaped intermediate rewards.

## Background: the baseline (level-1-learn)

A deterministic 4 by 4 GridWorld with a replay verifier and tabular
Q-learning. The value-iteration greedy policy takes 6 steps with return 4;
a seeded random policy over 200 episodes scores mean return -17.07 with
goal rate 0.085. The replay verifier graded 6 of 6 scenarios as expected.
The point taught here: a reward number is a claim, and the verifier
grades a recorded trajectory by replaying it. Full record:
level-1-learn/README.md and LEARN-SPEC.md.

## Method

Build (level-2-build): four environments (GridWorld, CliffGrid 3 by 5, a
three-arm deterministic bandit, a sequence-memory task), one replay
verifier each, and a reward-hacking post-mortem per environment. Record:
level-2-build/README.md and BUILD-SPEC.md.

Evaluate (level-3-deep): per task, a tabular softmax policy starts at
zero logits. Each iteration samples a group of 16 episodes, replay-verifies
each one, reads the reward from the verified trajectory, computes the
group-relative advantage (reward minus group mean, divided by the group's
population std; a zero-variance group makes no update), and takes the
softmax policy-gradient step. Evaluation: 500 sampled episodes per
policy, evaluation seeds 9101 to 9105. The seed sweep (run_seeds.py)
retrains every task over 10 training seeds and adds two baselines at the
same episode budget: REINFORCE with a mean baseline and no std
normalisation, and tabular Q-learning on the two grids. Record:
level-3-deep/README.md and DEEP-SPEC.md.

## Method, evidence and threats in detail

From the 2026-11-07 research review (formerly docs/research-review.md),
kept here as written apart from its letter grades, which are internal.
"This change" below means that 2026-11-07 change.

### Method

Each environment, verifier, sampler and update rule is a few dozen lines of stdlib Python with a
hand-computed oracle written first (LEARN-SPEC.md, BUILD-SPEC.md, DEEP-SPEC.md). The update is the
standard softmax policy gradient with a group-relative advantage, (r - mean) / std over a group of
16, and zero-variance groups make no update. The method is fully specified and deterministic.
Its main limit is uniform episodic credit, which DEEP-SPEC.md names as a failure mode.

### Baselines

Before: the only comparisons were a uniform random policy, value-iteration optimum and one
Q-learning seed per grid. Nothing tested whether the group-relative advantage itself helps.

Fixed in this change (`level-3-deep/run_seeds.py`, `results_seeds.json`):

- REINFORCE with a mean baseline and no std normalisation, same seeds, same lr, same group size,
  same episode budget (`tasks.*.reinforce_mean_baseline`).
- Tabular Q-learning at the same episode budget on both grids (`tasks.*.q_learning`).

Result: the two advantage rules are close where success is common (GridWorld mean success 0.9972
vs 0.9912, CliffGrid 0.9964 vs 0.9766, bandit 1.0 vs 0.9994) and far apart where success is rare
(sequence 0.97 vs 0.0254). Dividing by a small group std scales up the one success in a group,
which matters when success starts at (1/4)^4. Q-learning's greedy policy reaches the goal in 10 of
10 seeds on both grids (`q_learning.greedy_goal_seeds`). No Q baseline exists for the
bandit and sequence tasks, where the repo has no tabular Q code to reuse.

### Statistics and uncertainty

Before: every before/after delta came from one training run. pass@k used disjoint in-order blocks
of k and the README did not say how that differs from the usual estimator. No intervals.

Fixed in this change:

- 10 training seeds per task (seed i = recorded seed + 1000 i, so seed 0 is the recorded run),
  reported as mean, sample sd, min, max and a 95% Student t-interval (`tasks.*.grpo.success_rate`).
  The recorded single runs sit inside the seed spread; the widest spread is the sequence task, sd
  0.032069, min 0.88.
- The unbiased pass@k estimator 1 - C(n-c,k)/C(n,k) (Chen et al. 2021) computed next to the block
  version (`tasks.*.pass_at_k`). On these runs they agree to within 0.000004 because success is
  either very rare or nearly certain; the block version is kept because the recorded files use it.
- Per-iteration learning curves for the 4 runs and 2 ablations (`results_deep_curves.json`).
- A learning-rate by group-size grid at a fixed episode budget, 3 seeds per cell
  (`tasks.*.sensitivity`). The sequence task is the sensitive one: at lr 0.1 mean success falls
  from 0.53 (group 4) to 0.007 (group 32).

Evaluation uses the same five evaluation seeds (9101 to 9105) for every policy. That is
deliberate common random numbers: comparisons between conditions are paired on identical sampling
streams, which lowers the variance of the differences. The cost is that evaluation-sampling noise
is not averaged over in the absolute numbers; with 500 episodes per policy and success rates near
0 or 1, that noise is small next to the seed-to-seed spread reported above.

### Reproducibility

Strong before and stronger now. Every runner writes sorted JSON with no timing fields; two runs
are byte-identical and the README quotes the sha256 of each results file. CI now reruns all four
runners and fails on any diff (pinned to Python 3.12, because 3.12 changed float `sum()` to
compensated summation, which the runs depend on). The dashboard ports the code to JavaScript,
including CPython's Mersenne Twister seeding, and `site/src/rl.check.mjs` fails the site build
unless the port reproduces every recorded number, curve and policy table exactly.

### Threats to validity

Internal validity. The verifier was tested on 22 hand-picked scenarios. Fixed in this change:
`tests/test_verifier_fuzz.py` applies every single-field mutation (each action, each state, each
reward, the return, the goal and terminal flags) to 44 valid logs, 7,880 mutations in all, and
checks the verifier against an independent replay oracle. It accepts exactly the mutations that
are replay-consistent (an action that bumps the same wall, a goal flag under-claimed) and rejects
the rest.

The unexplained CliffGrid detour (greedy 8 steps, return 2, against an optimum of 6 steps,
return 4). Fixed by measurement (`tasks.cliffgrid-3x5.detour`): of 498 successful evaluation
episodes only 0.2% take 6 steps and 225 take 8. The success-bit reward pays every successful path
the same, so nothing in the objective prefers the short one. This is the credit-assignment limit
DEEP-SPEC.md predicted, now quantified.

Construct validity of "recall" on the sequence task. Fixed by measurement
(`tasks.sequence-memory-4.heldout_targets`): the trained policy's expected success is 0.983805 on
the trained target and 0.000064 averaged over the 255 other targets. It memorised one target. The
repo claims nothing more, and the dashboard now says so with the number.

External validity. Limited by design: tabular policy, four tiny deterministic tasks, no language
model, no KL term, no clipping, no reference policy. **Remaining; needs a GPU:** a GRPO run with a
KL penalty and ratio clipping on a small open language model (on the order of 0.5B to 1.5B
parameters) against verifier-scored tasks, which takes one GPU with at least 24 GB of memory for
tens of GPU-hours. backlog item 1.

Novelty, stated honestly: small. None of the components is new. The value is a fully transparent,
dependency-free, byte-reproducible miniature of the GRPO plus verifier loop, with the reward-hacking
attempts and the two failure modes recorded, and a browser port that provably reproduces it. That
is a teaching and reproducibility contribution, not a methods contribution. The
references are listed, but no systematic comparison against an existing environment library was
run.

### Limitations

Stated plainly in README.md, every level README, the backlog below and on the dashboard: teaching
analogue, tiny tasks, replay checks consistency not task correctness, two named hacks that replay
cannot see (a bandit log that rewrites arm and payoff together, and hard-coding the public
sequence target), uninformative groups on rare-success tasks (CliffGrid 223 of 300,
`results_deep.json` `tasks[1].train.uninformative_groups`).

## Limits named in the internal review

The internal review of each stage named these limits (the scores
themselves are internal):

- Learn: one tiny deterministic task, so every number proves the loop,
  not RL at scale; gamma fixed at 1.0 with no discount or
  stochastic-transition variant; Q-learning shown for one seed and one
  configuration; the verifier grades trajectories for this environment
  only.
- Build: four tiny deterministic tasks; deterministic bandit payoffs, so
  exploration is solved by construction and no regret claim is possible;
  the sequence target is fixed and public, so memorisation and general
  memory cannot be separated; Q-learning shown for one seed and one
  configuration; no stochastic-transition variant, so risk-sensitive
  behaviour is untested.
- Deep: teaching analogue only (tabular softmax, no LLM, no KL, clipping
  or critic); one training seed per task at v1.0 (closed on 2026-11-07
  by the 10-seed sweep); scoring is an in-repo implementation with no
  external harness; the CliffGrid greedy path is suboptimal and sampled
  grid returns sit below optimal; uniform step credit is named and
  measured, not fixed; uninformative-group waste is counted, not
  mitigated.

## Conclusion

A careful, honest teaching artefact. The answer to the question, on these
tasks: yes, a GRPO-style update trained only on replay-verifier rewards
solves all four tasks across 10 training seeds, and the std-normalised
advantage is what makes the rare-success sequence task learnable at this
budget (0.970 against 0.0254 for REINFORCE with a mean baseline). Two
reward definitions break it, both predicted and measured: a verifier
return that pays ending early in the cliff (ablation A), and a success
bit too rare to ever produce an informative group (ablation B). A correct
verifier does not imply a learnable reward. What remains needs a GPU or
an outside reviewer, and the backlog below says which. None of this is
confirmatory: it becomes so only under a locked proposal.

## v1.1 backlog

Formerly ROADMAP.md. v1.0 shipped 2026-11-05: Learn, Build, and compressed Deep (see
level-3-deep/DEEP-SPEC.md for what compressed Deep means here). This
list is the named remainder of the v2 Deep scope plus the fixes the
v1.0 internal review named. Nothing here is claimed as done.

1. Real LLM GRPO: fine-tune a small open model on verifier-scored
   tasks with a KL penalty and ratio clipping, on a free GPU route,
   and compare its before and after pass@k against this tabular
   analogue. This is the item that turns the teaching analogue into
   the real method.
2. P27 harness integration: when the agent-evals harness (P27)
   exists as a usable scorer, re-score the v1.0 policies through it
   and publish any differences against the in-repo P06-pattern
   scorer.
3. Training-seed variance: at least 5 training seeds per task, with
   success and mean return reported as a distribution, not one run.
   Done in this version: level-3-deep/run_seeds.py retrains every task
   over 10 training seeds and writes the distribution to
   level-3-deep/results_seeds.json.
4. Step-level credit: replace uniform episodic credit with a
   per-step advantage or a learned critic baseline, and re-measure
   the CliffGrid greedy detour (8 steps, return 2 in v1.0).
5. Uninformative-group mitigation: curriculum or success replay so
   rare-success tasks stop spending most groups at zero variance
   (CliffGrid spent 223 of 300 groups uninformative in v1.0).
6. Stochastic environments: add a slip probability variant of
   CliffGrid where the row-1 path beside the cliff is genuinely
   risky, and test whether the trained policy prices that risk.
7. Held-out sequence targets: train and evaluate the sequence task
   on targets never seen in training, to separate recall skill from
   memorisation of one public target (a limit named at Build).
8. Larger and combined tasks: bigger grids, combined
   navigate-then-recall tasks, and a task whose verifier checks a
   multi-step proof or program, closer to real RLVR workloads.
9. Entropy and temperature study: measure how an entropy bonus
   changes the uninformative-group rate and the cliff trap.
10. Reward-mode study, extended: per-step verifier rewards for the
    bandit and sequence tasks, scored against the episodic modes
    used in v1.0 and its two ablations.
